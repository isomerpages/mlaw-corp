Mr Ren Hongbin, Chairman of the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade (CCPIT)

Mr Wu Yanjun, Vice Minister of Justice of the People’s Republic of China

Mr Wang Zhenjiang, Vice President of the China Law Society

Mr Ren Jingdong, Vice Governor of Sichuan Province

Our many friends from China and colleagues from Singapore

Distinguished guests

Ladies and gentlemen

**Introduction**

1.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Good morning to all of you. Thank you very much for joining us here today.

2.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; It is a real pleasure to join you here at the 6th edition of the Singapore-China International Commercial Dispute Resolution Conference. As you heard from Chairman Ren earlier, this is the first time we are holding the conference in China outside Beijing, and so we are very glad to be in Sichuan, and in Chengdu in particular.

3.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; I want to also thank Chairman Ren and the CCPIT team, as well as the Sichuan Provincial Government and the Chengdu Municipal Government, for hosting us so warmly, so generously, and for making all the arrangements for us to have this event here this morning.

4.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; You have also warmly welcomed us to China many times. And I think besides the knowledge exchange and the know-how that we will speak about later this morning, what is important about this conference is also the depth of trust that we build between Singapore and China, between our lawyers, our officials, as well as our business people.

5.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; To understand the landscape of both countries, to understand how investments take place, and in the event that there is a dispute, to understand how that dispute can be resolved. So, this conference has really become an important platform for both our legal as well as business communities to exchange ideas, to exchange know-how, and most importantly, to expand and build on partnerships.

6.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; I have visited China many times, but this is my first time in Chengdu and in Sichuan. Of course, I have been introduced to the many aspects of Sichuan that Chairman Ren spoke about earlier. I visited Mount Qingcheng (青城山), and I have also sampled many of the delicacies on the street. The only thing that I have not tried yet is the one that I have not dared to try yet, which is the Lao Ma Tu Tou (老妈兔头).

7.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; I arrived in Chengdu three days ago from Shanghai. In Shanghai, I co-chaired the Singapore-Shanghai Comprehensive Cooperation Council meeting with Shanghai Mayor Zhu Zhongming.

8.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Travelling from Shanghai to Chengdu reminds me of the many dimensions and the deep layers that exist in the partnership between Singapore and China, as well as the opportunities to further build on our longstanding friendship.

**Chengdu**

9.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; In preparing for this speech, I did some homework, since I have not been to Chengdu – partly to understand Chengdu, but also because I come from Singapore and we love our food, to do research on what were the delicacies I have to try.

10.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; But getting to know a city that is so rich in culture, history and heritage, with more than 2,300 years of history, in just a few days is of course too ambitious.

11.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; But a few places caught my attention – Wuhou Shrine (武侯祠), Dujiangyan (都江堰), and Jiaozi Towers (交子双塔).

12.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The stories behind each of these places that I visited over the past few days reflect a storyline between our two countries, but also in the work we do and that we will be talking about as lawyers in resolving conflicts. So let me elaborate on these three places a little bit more.

**Wuhou Shrine**

13.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; First, Wuhou Shrine (武侯祠), which already, of course, has a very deep Singapore connection. As many of you know, Mr Lee Kuan Yew, our founding Prime Minister, visited in 1988, two years before our countries established diplomatic relations.

14.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; He was reportedly very taken by this couplet by Zhao Fan (赵藩):

能攻心则反侧自消，从古知兵非好战；

不审势即宽严皆误，后来治蜀要深思。

15.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; This has a very deep meaning for what we are trying to do today. One part simply says that without understanding the situation, you can get it wrong, even if you are strict or lenient. I think that wisdom applies to commercial disputes.

16.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; A good lawyer must understand both the law and the business, what the parties hope to achieve, what went wrong, and whether their relationships can be preserved.

17.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Across borders, all the more so. You must understand different legal systems, business practices and cultural contexts and nuances that make a big difference. That is why exchanges between our legal communities are so important.

18.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; We recently welcomed a Chengdu Lawyers Association delegation to Singapore. And today, I am here with many Singapore lawyers in Chengdu.

19.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; These are some examples of the kinds of exchanges that build understanding and trust. When a difficult issue arises, as we will always see in practice, it helps to have someone that you know, whose judgment you can respect, and whom you can simply call. Of course, in today’s context, you can send a message by WeChat or WhatsApp and contact them quickly. These are the kinds of contacts and networks that I hope that you can use on occasions like today to continue to nurture and to build.

**Dujiangyan**

20.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The second place I visited over the weekend was Dujiangyan (都江堰). It is remarkable that something that was thought about and built thousands of years ago remains in use today. It is a tremendous engineering marvel.

21.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; But what interested me most about it was the thinking. The people who were thinking about the problem then carefully studied the river flow, the sediment, and the surrounding land topography to understand the water levels at different times of the year, and then divided and channelled the water and put its force and flow to good use.

22.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The genius lay in understanding the river and in understanding how to work with it. I am sure I cannot do justice to the project, so I encourage my colleagues from Singapore who have not seen it to go and see it because it is not just an engineering marvel.

23.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; I think if you look back at history, it had a deep impact on how China developed and prospered over the years.

24.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; For the work that we do, there is a parallel with dispute resolution. Where there is a business, where there is a commercial venture, where there is investment, there will invariably be some level of differences. The question is how to manage them without creating bigger problems, spending more than the dispute is worth, or sometimes unnecessarily damaging a longstanding relationship simply because of some commercial differences.

25.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Different disputes will need different tools. Sometimes arbitration is the right approach. Sometimes litigation. Sometimes mediation between two parties will help them find a better solution.

26.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; In this way, Singapore and China have both continued to develop options for our users in different ways. We have both become a lot more sophisticated, a lot more mature, and I think we have benefitted from the learning that we have had from each other.

27.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; In Singapore, arbitration, mediation and international commercial litigation are supported by institutions like the Singapore International Arbitration Centre (SIAC), the Singapore International Mediation Centre (SIMC) and the Singapore International Commercial Court (SICC).

28.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The real test is whether these options work for the businesses. How do they work, and can they operate sometimes in tandem to resolve business disputes? Whether parties can choose a suitable process to resolve their differences or not, in many ways, relies on the institutional depth and the presence that we build in these institutions.

**Jiaozi Towers**

29.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The third place that I visited was Jiaozi Towers (交子双塔), whose name recalls a much older innovation. Before this visit, I did not know that Chengdu was widely regarded as the birthplace of the world’s first paper money.

30.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; During the Song Dynasty, Sichuan’s iron coins were very heavy to carry around, and as trade grew, merchants began to carry more and more iron coins. It became more and more difficult, and some issuers could not meet their obligations. Disputes followed. Eventually, the authorities established an official Jiaozi (交子) office in Chengdu in 1023, many years ago.

31.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Of course, the original office no longer stands, so I took the occasion to visit the Jiaozi Towers. Not quite the Song Dynasty but it is a fitting reminder of an idea that was born here, more than a thousand years ago, where innovation met a business need, but brought new questions of responsibility and trust.

32.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; As I reflect on the topics that I saw would be discussed today in the panels, I think the lesson remains relevant. As businesses change, the law must keep evolving, keep innovating, and keep changing.

33.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; China-ASEAN trade crossed US$1 trillion for the first time in 2025, reaching approximately US$1.05 trillion in that year. China has been ASEAN’s largest trading partner for 17 consecutive years, while ASEAN has been China’s largest trading partner for six consecutive years.

34.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; This tells us something about the growing relationship between China and ASEAN, and Singapore in particular, as a gateway to ASEAN. This relationship now extends into the digital and green economies, as well as supply chain connectivity.

35.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; More businesses, more contracts, and now more assets span borders, bringing complex issues. Commerce, of course, has changed since the original days of Jiaozi (交子), but businesses will still need new ways of making payments and undertaking transactions.

36.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; So that is something we should think about today, as we have dialogues and share thoughts on the panels about how we can build on the growing Singapore-China and China-ASEAN trade levels, and move into different jurisdictions to expand our trade options.

**Singapore and China: Working Together**

37.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; As Chinese businesses expand across Southeast Asia, I think that Singapore’s lawyers can play that role well. Our lawyers are accustomed to working across different markets, different legal systems. Chinese lawyers bring deep knowledge of their clients, of their industries, and of course, of their home market, which is huge.

38.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; A company entering a new market will need both areas of expertise. The same is true when a dispute arises as well, and these are opportunities to build the connections. As I said today, on occasions like these, I hope that you can use the opportunity to expand on those connections.

39.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; In fact, these connections are best built before they are needed, before disputes arise, or before your clients need &nbsp;to move across different jurisdictions.

**MinLaw and CCPIT**

40.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; So, my final point today is to look at these connections. Alongside commercial connections, exchanges between our governments, of course, allow us to learn from one another even more. My Ministry of Law (MinLaw) and China's Ministry of Justice have a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) providing a foundation for cooperation, and we, of course, look forward to expanding this cooperation even more.

41.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Our partnership with CCPIT goes back a long way, and it reflects a very deep and shared commitment to turning discussions into practical cooperation. In our discussions that take place right across the year, not just at an annual occasion like this, we make sure that whatever we discuss, we put into the pipeline for fruition, for reality.

42.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The MOU that Chairman Ren and I signed earlier this year in May in Beijing reflects that cooperation. It has broadened the areas of our cooperation beyond dispute resolution, to move into international trade and compliance, intellectual property, the digital as well as green economies. So what began some years ago as a dispute resolution conference has now grown into a broader partnership that serves our businesses as well as our communities internationally.

43.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; And I want to say a big thank you to Chairman Ren, my friend. His warm approach and his personal touches, make all the difference. He has always been open to ideas, open to exchanges, and always has an eye on what we can do to help our businesses, both in China and in Singapore, and mutually benefit from our cooperation and from our networks.

44.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Chairman Ren, 谢谢你. Thank you very much for your friendship, and for your strong support. I greatly treasure not just the hosting of this occasion, but the meaning behind it, the trust that we have built behind it, and we look forward to bringing this partnership even further.

**Conclusion**

45.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; At the 5th edition last year in Singapore, we presented our guests with titanium chopsticks. Those of you who use chopsticks will know it is never possible to just use one chopstick.

46.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; When we gave the chopsticks, we also had a small poem:

一双筷子，相辅相成，象征同心共赢；

钛合金身，坚韧不屈，寓意合作长青。

愿我们如钛筷相倚，携手共进，行稳致远。

47.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; I think those words capture the spirit of this conference. Like chopsticks, we cannot operate alone. We can achieve more together.

48.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; We bring different strengths, different perspectives and experiences. But what matters is the deep level of understanding and trust between one another, and knowing how to work with one another.

49.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; My hope for this conference is quite simple. I hope that we can turn introductions into friendships, friendships into partnerships, and partnerships into real opportunities for businesses and for professionals across Singapore and China.

50.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; These are the people that we serve ultimately, and through this collaboration, I think we can bring benefit to the greater good, both in Singapore and in China.

51.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; On that note, I thank all of you for being here. We welcome all of you to Singapore next year, for the seventh edition, and we hope that we will be able to match the hospitality and the level of organisation that has been shown to us by our hosts this year.

52.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Thank you very much.
