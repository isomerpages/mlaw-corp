24 November 2026 Posted in \[Press releases\](/news/press-releases)

1.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Minister for Law and Second Minister for Home Affairs Edwin Tong SC and Senior Parliamentary Secretary, Ministry of Law and Ministry of Social and Family Development Eric Chua made an official visit to Chengdu, Sichuan Province, from 22 to 24 November 2026 to participate in the 6th Singapore–China International Commercial Dispute Resolution Conference on 23 November 2026.

2.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Co-organised by Singapore’s Ministry of Law (MinLaw), the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade (CCPIT) and the International Commercial Dispute Prevention and Settlement Organization (ICDPASO), the Conference was held in western China for the first time. CCPIT is China’s national trade and investment promotion agency.

3.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Minister Tong and CCPIT Chairman Ren Hongbin opened the Conference together with China’s Vice Minister of Justice Wu Yanjun, China Law Society Vice President Wang Zhenjiang and Sichuan Vice Governor Ren Jingdong. SPS Chua and Liu Jiannan, Vice Chairman of CCPIT and Chairman of ICDPASO, delivered the closing remarks.

4.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The Conference drew about 400 participants, including government officials, legal and business professionals, academics and representatives of dispute resolution institutions. Close to 30 travelled from Singapore, reflecting their interest in building closer ties with the legal and business communities in western China.

**Building on the longstanding partnership between MinLaw and CCPIT**

5.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; MinLaw initiated the Conference with CCPIT in Beijing in 2019 to connect Singapore law firms, practitioners and dispute resolution institutions with their Chinese counterparts and the Chinese business community. At the 2nd edition in 2022, which drew more than two million views across its livestreaming platforms, Minister Tong and Chairman Ren agreed to hold the Conference annually, alternating between Singapore and China. Singapore hosted the 5th edition in 2025 as the two countries marked 35 years of diplomatic relations. On that occasion, MinLaw, CCPIT and eight other institutions from Singapore and China signed a Joint Declaration on Cooperation.

6.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; MinLaw and CCPIT have since also broadened their partnership beyond the Conference. In May 2026, they signed a Memorandum of Understanding on Legal and Dispute Resolution Cooperation, covering international trade policy and compliance, intellectual property management, and legal issues arising in the digital and green economies.

**Strengthening ties with Sichuan**

7.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; During the visit, Minister Tong and SPS Chua also met Vice Minister of Justice Wu, as well as Sichuan provincial leadership, Sichuan Governor Shi Xiaolin, Vice Governor Ren Jingdong and Chengdu Mayor Chen Shuping.

8.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; On 24 November, Minister Tong spoke at an event jointly organised by the Law Society of Singapore (LawSoc), the Singapore International Arbitration Centre (SIAC) and the Chengdu Lawyers Association (CDLA). The event brought together lawyers from both Chengdu and Singapore, to jointly collaborate on the promotion of exchanges as well as deeper commercial connections. Minister Tong and SPS Chua witnessed the signing of two Memoranda of Understanding – one between CDLA and LawSoc and the other between CDLA and SIAC. Both agreements aim to promote professional exchanges and closer cooperation.

9.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Mr See Chern Yang, Co-Chairperson of LawSoc Foreign Bar Liaison Committee (China Chapter) and Director, Dispute Resolution at Drew &amp; Napier LLC, said, “The close collaboration between the Ministry of Law and the Law Society has been instrumental in building bridges between legal communities and creating new opportunities for Singapore lawyers abroad. Minister Tong's support for the MOU signing between the Law Society and the Chengdu Lawyers Association reflects a shared vision of deeper Singapore-China legal cooperation, greater professional exchange, and stronger support for businesses operating across borders. I am looking forward to the continued collaboration between the Ministry of Law and the Law Society in strengthening legal cooperation and expanding professional opportunities for lawyers from both jurisdictions.”

10.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; These legal ties build on a longstanding economic relationship. This year marks the 30th anniversary of the Singapore–Sichuan Trade and Investment Committee, the second-oldest of Singapore’s eight provincial business councils in China and the only one in western China. By 2025, Singapore investors had established about 900 enterprises in Sichuan, while Sichuan companies had established about 80 enterprises in Singapore.

11.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Sichuan is western China’s largest provincial economy, with a GDP of RMB6.8 trillion in 2025. Chengdu, its capital, accounted for 36.6% of that total. More than 300 Fortune Global 500 companies have established a presence in the province. Sichuan companies are also using Singapore as a base to expand into Southeast Asia. Singapore’s legal and dispute resolution professionals can help these businesses enter new markets, protect their intellectual property across jurisdictions and resolve disputes with regional partners.

12.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Minister Tong said, “Our cooperation with CCPIT is a valued one. It goes beyond commercial co-operation, to building a deep and sustainable reservoir of goodwill, trust and understanding. In a more divided and fractured world, these ties are invaluable. We are also delighted that the Conference is being held in western China so that our lawyers and dispute resolution institutions can establish new networks, and foster stronger collaboration with businesses and lawyers in Sichuan. I look forward to working further with CCPIT and our partners in Sichuan to make it easier for businesses expanding into Southeast Asia to be supported by top class legal and dispute resolution services that they need.”
